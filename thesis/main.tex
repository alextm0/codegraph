\chapter*{Abstract}
\vspace{0.5cm}
\hrule
\vspace{0.5cm}

The effectiveness of AI-driven software engineering agents is often limited by the quality of the retrieved code context. Current retrieval methods typically rely on pure lexical matching or dense embeddings, which frequently fail to capture the complex, multi-file structural dependencies of large repositories. This thesis introduces CodeGraph, a hybrid retrieval system that pairs lexical seed extraction with structural graph traversal. By modeling a repository as a directed dependency graph and employing the Personalized PageRank algorithm to propagate relevance from task-specific starting points, we provide agents with a deterministic, structurally-grounded view of the codebase.

Empirical evaluation on the SWE-bench Lite dataset shows that CodeGraph achieves a 74.0\% Recall@10 rate. This result represents a 32 percentage point improvement over lexical baselines. Our analysis identifies initial seed extraction as the primary bottleneck in repository-level retrieval. We demonstrate that systematic improvements to seed quality are more effective than increasing graph density. These results confirm that explicit structural context allows AI agents to navigate codebases through deterministic paths rather than ambiguous search.
